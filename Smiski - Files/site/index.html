Model 1 · supervised regression

What drives a figure's resale premium?

Target: price above retail, in percent. Features: series theme, rarity tier, retail price, release season, months since launch, collab flag and search interest at launch. Validation holds out whole series, because figures in one series look alike and a random split would leak.

Model vs baselines

Grey bars are baselines: the training mean, and the mean premium per rarity tier. Beating the second one is the real test.

What moves the prediction (SHAP)

SHAP explains the model, not the market: it shows which inputs the model leans on, not what causes prices. Retail price partly stands in for release era.

    Model 2 · time series

    Where is demand heading, and when did the craze change gear?

    Weekly search interest for "smiski". Six forecasters compete against two naive baselines. The winner was chosen on rolling backtests, then scored once on the final 52 weeks it never saw.

     

    Dotted verticals mark where the hold-out starts and where the data ends. The 26-week forecast assumes no new series launch.

    Hold-out scorecard

    MAE in index points on the last 52 weeks. MASE is scaled by in-sample one-step naive error, so long-horizon values sit well above 1. Coverage should be near 80%.

    Changepoints vs product events

    PELT on the de-seasonalised log series. A break near an event is consistent with it mattering, not proof.

        Assumptions in the real tables

          How to read this honestly

          • Baselines first. Every model is shown next to a naive one, with the improvement and its uncertainty.
          • Small data. There are only 18 series; they, not rows, are the independent units. Prefer simple models and treat near-ties as ties.
          • Uncertainty everywhere. Intervals for prices and forecasts, bootstrap bands for importance and correlation.
          • No official art. Figures here are original placeholder sprites. Series and figure names and the short descriptions are quoted from the official site; no images are republished.
          • Correlation, not causation. SHAP, changepoints and lead/lag describe patterns.
